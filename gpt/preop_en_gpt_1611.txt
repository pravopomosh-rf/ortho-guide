preop_en_S8 | Eliquis, Xarelto, Pradaxa and Coumadin reduce blood clotting. Before some operations, they may need to be stopped temporarily. This is particularly important when spinal or epidural anesthesia is planned. Bleeding near the spinal nerves can cause serious damage. There is no single safe stopping interval for every patient. The plan depends on the medicine, its dose, kidney function, the type of surgery and the planned anesthetic procedure. The anesthesiologist and the prescribing doctor will decide when to stop and restart the medicine. In a few patients at particularly high risk of blood clots, the doctor may prescribe a temporary anticoagulant injection, such as enoxaparin, while Coumadin is withheld. This is called bridging and is not needed for most patients. It is not normally recommended when Eliquis, Xarelto or Pradaxa are interrupted. If you have a stent in a heart artery, tell your doctor. Medicines such as aspirin and clopidogrel help prevent blood clots, and stopping them can be dangerous. Do not stop these medicines without a plan agreed with your doctors, which may involve a cardiologist. Never change or stop your treatment on your own.

preop_en_S9 | Fasting and the stress of surgery can change blood sugar levels, so diabetes medicines need to be planned before surgery. Metformin is often not taken on the day of surgery. Your blood sugar will be checked regularly, and the treatment will be adjusted according to the results. If you have type one diabetes, you must continue to receive insulin even while fasting. This may involve an adjusted dose of basal insulin or a planned intravenous insulin infusion. Never stop insulin or change the dose on your own.
