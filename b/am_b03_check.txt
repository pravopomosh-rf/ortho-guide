001	If you have ever had an allergy to chlorhexidine or other ingredients in the product, do not use the wipes and tell the staff ahead of time. If you feel burning, itching, a rash, or redness, stop the cleaning and tell the staff right away. If the product gets into your eyes, rinse them right away with cool water and get medical help. If it gets into your ears or mouth, tell the staff right away so it can be rinsed out safely. If your face or throat swell, or you have trouble breathing, call the staff right away; outside the hospital, call an ambulance on 101.

006	What to bring with you
007	Bring a photo ID and the documents listed in your invitation for surgery. If the operation is arranged under a health fund (kupat holim) commitment, check ahead of time that you have a valid Form 17 with the correct medical service code. Ask at the registration desk whether this document is needed.
008	A list of your medicines, medical records, and test results.
009	Glasses, hearing aid, and dentures — in cases labeled with your name; a CPAP machine if you use one.
010	Comfortable clothes and closed, non-slip shoes; a walker or crutches if you were told to bring them.
011	It is best to leave valuables and large amounts of money at home.
012	ID wristband
013	When you are admitted, you will be given an ID wristband. Before medical procedures, staff will confirm at least two identifiers, such as your full name together with your date of birth or Israeli ID number, according to hospital rules (Israel Ministry of Health, circular 33/2006; WHO, 2007). If the details are wrong or the wristband is lost, tell the nurse right away.
014	Smoking and alcohol
015	If you smoke or drink alcohol, talk with your doctor about how to cut down or stop before surgery (NICE NG180).
016	When to tell the ward before surgery
017	Call the ward if before surgery you get a fever, a cough, a wound or rash on the skin in the area of the surgery, or if you become ill. The surgery may need to be postponed. If your life is in danger, call an ambulance on 101 (Magen David Adom).

036	The essentials in 30 seconds
037	On admission, a nurse will assess you and a doctor will examine you. If surgery is planned, an anaesthetist will also assess you before the operation. Some assessments may take place later.
038	Bring your documents, medical reports and an up-to-date list of all medicines and supplements you take. If you do not have a list, show the medicine packages to the staff.
039	Ask questions whenever something is unclear. Tell the staff if you need an interpreter or communication support.
040	Your nursing admission assessment
041	The nurse will ask about medical conditions, medicines, allergies, previous operations and anaesthesia, how you move around at home and what assistance you need.
042	The information will be recorded in your electronic medical record. Access is limited to authorised staff according to their duties.
043	Your height and weight will be measured if needed. The doses of some medicines depend on your weight, so it is important to provide an accurate weight.
044	Tell the staff about any special needs, such as being a Holocaust survivor, hearing or vision difficulties, significant anxiety, communication support or special dietary requirements.
045	You will receive an identification wristband. Do not remove it yourself. Tell the staff if the details are incorrect or the wristband causes discomfort.
046	Vital signs
047	The nurse will measure your blood pressure, pulse, temperature and blood oxygen saturation. Oxygen saturation is usually measured using a small sensor placed on your finger.
048	These measurements will be repeated according to your condition and monitoring plan, sometimes including overnight.
049	Screening for resistant bacteria
050	On admission, you may have screening swabs for bacteria resistant to certain antibiotics, such as CPE and VRE. Screening is carried out according to infection prevention guidance and ward procedures.
051	These tests usually involve a rectal swab. The procedure is brief but may cause mild discomfort. Staff will explain how it is performed.
052	A positive result may indicate that you carry the bacteria without having an active infection. Additional precautions may be needed, including a separate room or other suitable accommodation. See the
053	'CPE and VRE'
054	page for more information.
055	Your hospital room
056	There is a nurse call button beside your bed. Use it whenever you need help, especially before getting out of bed for the first time.
057	The bed position can be adjusted. Ask the staff to show you how to use the controls. Bed rails are used when needed following a safety assessment.
058	Staff will show you the toilet and shower and explain how to wash safely. You may be asked to use a special skin cleanser before surgery. After surgery, do not wet your dressing without permission.
059	There is a drinking water dispenser on the ward, and staff will show you where it is. Before surgery, follow the specific instructions you have been given about eating and drinking.
060	You will be given a hospital gown and a towel. Staff will advise you on what else you should bring.
061	Meeting the ward doctor
062	The ward doctor will examine you and review your medical records and test results. If surgery is planned, you will receive an explanation of its purpose, expected benefits, main risks and possible alternatives.
063	Before surgery, you will be asked to provide written informed consent. Sign only after receiving clear explanations and answers to your questions. Request an interpreter if needed.
064	Meeting the anaesthetist
065	The anaesthetist will assess your condition and discuss the appropriate type of anaesthesia. Options may include general, spinal or other regional anaesthesia, sometimes in combination.
066	The assessment may take place in advance, on the ward or in the preoperative area. The anaesthetist will explain the risks and ask for your informed consent to anaesthesia.
