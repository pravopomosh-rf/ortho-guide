023	Video: Laminectomy — what it is and how to prepare
024	Laminectomy is a spine operation in which part of the bony arch of a vertebra (the lamina) is removed and, if needed, thickened ligaments and bony overgrowths are removed too. This creates more room for compressed nerve structures. In lumbar stenosis, the nerve roots are mainly freed; in other parts of the spine, the decompression may be aimed at the spinal cord.
025	It is most often done for stenosis (narrowing) of the spinal canal in the lumbar spine, when pain, numbness, or weakness in the legs does not go away with treatment without surgery (Covaro et al., 2016).
026	Sometimes, along with freeing the nerves, the vertebrae also need to be fixed together (spinal fusion), for example when the spine is unstable. The surgeon decides after your examination whether you need it.
027	Getting up and walking usually start early, under the supervision of the ward team. The team decides exactly when.

037	Blood thinners
038	(anticoagulants and antiplatelet drugs): stop them only if your doctor tells you to.
039	If you take SGLT2 inhibitors (for example, empagliflozin, dapagliflozin, or ertugliflozin), tell your doctor in advance, even if you do not have diabetes: some of these medicines are also prescribed for heart failure and kidney disease. Before planned surgery, empagliflozin and dapagliflozin are usually stopped at least 3 days ahead, and ertugliflozin at least 4 days ahead. This helps lower the risk of ketoacidosis, which can happen without a marked rise in blood sugar. Your doctor decides when to stop and restart treatment. Do not change your treatment on your own.
040	Insulin
041	and other diabetes medicines: your doctor will give you an individual plan for the day before surgery and the day of surgery.
042	If you have type 1 diabetes, your body needs basal insulin. It may be given by injections or continuously through an insulin pump. Do not stop your basal insulin or change your pump settings on your own. Ask your doctor for an individual plan before and after surgery.

048	Take care of the tube:
049	do not pull on it, and make sure it does not kink. Do not insert or connect the tube yourself.
050	Tell the nurse right away if the tube falls out or comes disconnected; the container quickly fills with bright red blood; drainage that was there before suddenly stops; fluid leaks around the tube; clear, watery fluid appears in the container or on the dressing, especially if you also get a headache that gets worse when sitting or standing; pain or swelling increases; or you get a fever, dizziness, or new weakness or numbness in your legs. Clear discharge does not necessarily mean a leak of spinal fluid, but it must be checked. Do not try to clear, flush, or connect the tube yourself.
