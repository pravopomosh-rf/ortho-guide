001	Pain is treated right away and checked regularly. If the pain gets worse, ask for pain relief. If ordinary medicines are not enough, a nerve block (a pain-relieving injection near a nerve) may be used.
002	This time is used to check test results and, if needed, to get the body ready for surgery.
003	Source: NICE CG124, 1.3; AAOS 2021
004	Skin preparation before surgery
005	Before surgery, staff will help clean your skin. Depending on hospital rules, special antiseptic wipes or other products may be used for this. This cleaning lowers the number of germs on the skin, but on its own it does not guarantee that infection will be prevented.
006	Tell the staff if you are allergic to soap, antiseptics, or chlorhexidine.
007	Do not shave the skin in the area of the surgery yourself.
008	Tell the staff if you have a wound, redness, or skin irritation.
009	Source: WHO (SSI); CDC, 2017
010	Why the Clexane injection
011	After a hip fracture, the risk of blood clots goes up. So the medical team assesses the risk of clots and of bleeding and prescribes prevention. One of the medicines is enoxaparin (Clexane). It may be given before surgery if the surgery is postponed; the timing of the dose depends on the surgery plan and the anesthesia.
012	If spinal anesthesia is planned, there usually needs to be at least 12 hours between a preventive dose of enoxaparin and the injection in the back. After a treatment (higher) dose, the interval is at least 24 hours. If kidney function is reduced or there are other risk factors, the anesthesiologist may require extra safety measures. The surgery can also be done under general anesthesia.
013	After surgery, clot prevention is restarted when it is safe. If spinal anesthesia was used, the first preventive dose of enoxaparin is given no earlier than 12 hours after the spinal injection; the length of the surgery, the risk of bleeding, and whether there is a catheter are also taken into account. The medical team decides the exact timing.
014	If you take blood-thinning medicines at home, be sure to tell your doctor.
015	Source: AAOS 2021; NICE NG89; ASRA 2018 and 2025; Lovenox label (FDA)
016	Food, drink, and medicines before surgery
017	Because the surgery is urgent, its exact time may change. Do not eat or drink without permission from the medical team. Be sure to tell them when you last ate or drank. If the surgery is postponed, ask the staff when you are allowed to drink and how fluids will be given to you so that you do not get dehydrated.
018	Tell the doctors about all your medicines
019	, especially blood thinners: warfarin (Coumadin), apixaban (Eliquis), rivaroxaban (Xarelto), dabigatran (Pradaxa), clopidogrel (Plavix). They affect blood clotting and the choice of anesthesia; sometimes you need to wait before surgery or their effect needs to be adjusted.
020	Be sure to tell the doctor if you take insulin or other diabetes medicines, especially empagliflozin or dapagliflozin. Say when you took the last dose. For urgent surgery, the doctor will decide which medicines to hold for now, how to monitor blood sugar, and whether ketones need to be checked. Some medicines raise the risk of a dangerous complication — ketoacidosis — even when blood sugar is not very high. Do not take or stop medicines on your own.
021	Family members:
022	bring a list of all the medicines the patient takes at home (or the packages themselves).
023	Source: ASA 2017 and 2023; NICE CG124, 1.2.2; AAOS 2021; ADA 2026, section 16
024	Types of surgery
025	For a hip fracture without displacement, the surgeon may suggest fixing the bone with screws or a special metal device. Sometimes replacing part of the joint is considered. For a displaced femoral neck fracture in older patients, a partial or total joint replacement is usually recommended. A total replacement is considered based on how well you moved before, your general health, and how independent you are expected to be after recovery. A trochanteric fracture is a different type of fracture of the upper thigh bone, not a femoral neck fracture. It may be fixed with a plate with a sliding screw or an intramedullary nail (a rod inside the bone); for unstable types a nail is usually used. The final choice is made by the surgeon.

029	When the surgery happens
030	The aim is to do the surgery as early as possible — usually on the day of admission or the next day (NICE), within 24–48 hours (AAOS).
031	In Israel, the Ministry of Health monitors the percentage of patients who undergo surgery for a hip fracture within 48 hours of being admitted as inpatients. The nationwide figure was 89% in 2024.
032	If dangerous health problems are found, the team gives the needed treatment and preparation at the same time, trying to avoid unjustified delay of the surgery.
033	Source: NICE CG124, 1.2; AAOS 2021; Israel Ministry of Health, report 2013–2024
034	Getting up:
035	usually the day after surgery, with a physical therapist, if your condition allows. Early movement helps prevent blood clots, pneumonia, and pressure sores.
036	Putting weight on the leg:
037	after many hip fracture operations, you may put as much of your body weight on the operated leg as your pain allows. You do not need to push down forcefully. Ask your doctor how much weight bearing is allowed for you.
038	After a joint replacement
039	, special precautions when sitting, bending, and sleeping may be needed (usually for the first 6 weeks) so that the implant does not come out of the socket (dislocate). They depend on the surgical approach — the surgeon and physical therapist will explain which ones you need (AAOS OrthoInfo).
040	Pain relief:
041	regular, so that you can move and do your exercises.
042	After surgery, clot prevention may continue after you leave the hospital. For hip fractures, injections of medicines that reduce blood clotting are often used. According to NICE, prevention is usually prescribed for a month if the risk of clots is greater than the risk of bleeding. The doctor decides the specific medicine and how long to take it. Do not stop the prescribed treatment on your own.
043	A team works on your recovery: the surgeon, an internist or geriatrician, nurses, a physical therapist, and an occupational therapist.
044	Before discharge, talk with your doctor about testing for osteoporosis, ways to prevent falls, and the plan for further rehabilitation.
045	Source: NICE CG124, 1.6–1.8; NICE NG89, 1.11.2; AAOS 2021; ACCP 2012
046	Confusion (delirium) and how family members can help
047	In older people, sudden confusion can appear after a fracture and surgery: the person mixes up time and place and becomes restless or, on the contrary, sleepy. Confusion can begin even before the surgery. It is usually temporary, but sometimes it lasts longer and always needs to be assessed by the medical team. Tell the staff about it right away.
048	Bring glasses, a hearing aid, and dentures.
