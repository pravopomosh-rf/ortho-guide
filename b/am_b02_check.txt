001	Preparing for Surgery
002	Key points in 30 seconds
003	Follow your fasting instructions. The ward will confirm the exact times.
004	Do not stop or change medicines yourself; bring a full list of them.
005	Shower and wash your hair the evening before. Do not shave the surgical area.
006	Video
007	Video in development — it will appear here soon.
008	Who this leaflet is for
009	For patients who have a planned (elective) operation on the orthopaedic and spine surgery ward, and for their family members. This is general information: the individual instructions from your doctor, anesthesiologist, and ward are more important.
010	Food and drink before surgery
011	Before anesthesia and sedation, it is important to follow the fasting instructions you are given: this lowers the risk of stomach contents getting into the airway. The time intervals are counted back from the start of anesthesia or the procedure. If you have diabetes, slow emptying of the stomach, or other risk factors, the anesthesiologist may give you a different plan. Follow the individual instructions from the ward.
012	General rules for adults without increased risk (ASA, 2017):
013	a light meal (for example, toast and tea) — no later than 6 hours before;
014	fatty or fried food, meat — no later than 8 hours before;
015	clear liquids (water, tea or coffee without milk, clear juice without pulp) — no later than 2 hours before;
016	milk (not breast milk) counts as food — no later than 6 hours before.
017	In some cases, clear carbohydrate drinks are allowed before surgery. You should not take special pre-surgery drinks on your own: follow the written instructions from the anesthesiologist and the ward (ASA, 2023).
018	Medicines
019	Do not stop or change your medicines on your own.
020	Bring a full list of your medicines, including vitamins and herbal products (NICE NG5).
021	Get separate instructions ahead of time for each medicine you take regularly: take it in the morning, skip it for now, or change the dose. It is especially important to agree on blood pressure medicines, diabetes medicines, and medicines that affect blood clotting. Morning pills that are allowed can be taken with a small sip of water (AHA/ACC, 2024).
022	If you take medicines that reduce blood clotting (such as warfarin, apixaban, rivaroxaban, dabigatran, clopidogrel, or aspirin), ask your doctor in advance whether any need to be stopped and when. Some, including aspirin in certain situations, may be continued. Do not stop them yourself. Your doctor will also decide whether any temporary replacement treatment is needed.
023	If you take empagliflozin, dapagliflozin, or canagliflozin, tell your doctor ahead of time. According to FDA labels, these are usually stopped at least 3 days before planned surgery; ertugliflozin at least 4 days. Restarting the treatment is agreed with your doctor once your condition is stable and you are eating normally again.
024	If you take semaglutide, tirzepatide, or other medicines for diabetes or weight loss, tell the anesthesiologist. Preparation and whether the treatment needs to be changed for a while are decided individually. Do not stop medicines on your own.
025	Showering and skin care before surgery
026	On the evening before a planned operation, take a shower with soap and wash your hair. Put on clean clothes. If possible, shower again on the morning of the surgery (Israel Ministry of Health, circular 24/2001).
027	What to wash with is in the section below.
028	Do not shave the area of the surgery: shaving raises the risk of infection. If hair needs to be removed, the hospital will do it with an electric clipper on the day of surgery (NICE NG125).
029	Take off jewelry, nail polish and artificial nails, and makeup.
030	What to wash with on the evening before and the morning of surgery
031	Illustration: on the left, a cleansing product with 4% chlorhexidine; on the right, wipes with 2% chlorhexidine (wipes are covered below).
032	The ward may give you an antiseptic cleansing product with 4% chlorhexidine, for example "Septal Scrub" (Teva). If you are given it, wash with it twice: on the evening before surgery and on the morning of surgery (following the manufacturer's instructions).
033	How: first wash your hair with regular shampoo, and your face and outer genital area with regular soap. Rinse well. Then apply about 25 ml of the undiluted product to wet, unbroken skin and wash your body from the neck to the feet, avoiding the genitals, mucous membranes, wounds, and irritated skin. Rinse well with water, dry off with a clean towel, and put on clean clothes.
034	Do not let the product get into your eyes or ears, do not use it inside the nose, ears, or mouth, and do not swallow it. If it gets into your eyes, rinse them right away with plenty of water. If you have pain, redness, or vision problems, get medical help urgently.
035	If you have ever had an allergy to chlorhexidine or other ingredients in the product, do not use it and tell the staff. If you get itching, a rash, or redness, stop using it, rinse it off, and contact the ward or your doctor right away. If your lips or throat swell, or you have trouble breathing, call an ambulance on 101 right away. If you are already in the hospital, call the staff urgently.
036	If you were not given this product, take a regular shower with soap.
037	For use on the skin only. Keep out of reach of children.

042	Wipes with 2% chlorhexidine: who gets them and how they are used
043	Before some operations, for example spine surgery, staff may also clean the skin with wipes containing 2% chlorhexidine (for example, Sage, Stryker). If the patient showered first, the skin must be completely dry. If the patient cannot shower, staff help with a hygiene wash in bed and, if needed, do the pre-surgery skin cleaning separately. The ward decides according to its own protocol.
044	Diagram: front (zones 1–5) and back (zone 6). The head and face are not cleaned with the wipes.
045	The package contains 6 wipes. The diagram shows an example of a six-zone body-cleansing sequence. The hospital staff will follow the ward's protocol. This is not the manufacturer's (Sage) instruction for cleaning the surgical area. The staff decide the exact scheme and the number of wipes needed.
046	neck, chest, abdomen, and groin folds
047	right shoulder, arm, armpit, and hand
048	left shoulder, arm, armpit, and hand
049	right leg and foot
050	left leg and foot
051	back of the neck, back, and buttocks
052	The skin is cleaned one zone after another, using a new wipe for each zone. Use back-and-forth motions, pressing firmly enough but not so hard that it hurts the skin. Pay special attention to skin folds.
053	Do not wipe the face, eyes, ears, mouth, genitals, mucous membranes, wounds, or damaged skin with the wipes.
054	After wiping, do not rinse the product off or dry it with a towel: the skin dries by itself (1–2 minutes) and may feel a little sticky — this is normal. Then put on a clean shirt and use clean sheets on the bed. Do not put on your own creams, lotions, or deodorant afterward unless the staff allow it.
