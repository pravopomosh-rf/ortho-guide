004	It is most often done for a compression fracture caused by osteoporosis.

006	Video: Kyphoplasty — what it is and how to prepare
007	Kyphoplasty is a minimally invasive procedure for a vertebral compression fracture, most often caused by osteoporosis. Through a small puncture, guided by X-ray imaging, a balloon is placed inside the body of the broken vertebra and inflated. This creates a cavity and can sometimes partly restore the height of the vertebra. The balloon is then deflated and removed, and the cavity is filled with bone cement, which hardens.
008	The goal is to reduce pain and strengthen the broken vertebra.
009	Kyphoplasty may be considered if severe pain continues after a recent osteoporotic vertebral fracture that has not healed, despite appropriate pain treatment. Examination and imaging must confirm that the pain comes from the fractured vertebra (NICE TA279).
010	The anesthesiologist chooses the type of anesthesia (general anesthesia, or local anesthesia with sedation).
011	Kyphoplasty does not treat osteoporosis itself. After the procedure, talk with your doctor about testing and about starting or continuing osteoporosis treatment to lower the risk of new fractures.

014	Drain
015	A drain is not usually needed after routine kyphoplasty. If you do have one, do not touch or disconnect the tube — the team will explain how to look after it.
016	In the hospital, tell the nurse right away. After you go home, go to the emergency department (ER) immediately if you develop new or worsening weakness or numbness in your legs, numbness around the genitals, between the legs, or around the anus (saddle area), difficulty starting or passing urine, loss of the usual feeling that you need to urinate, inability to urinate, or new loss of bladder or bowel control. Do not drive yourself. If you cannot get to the ER safely, call an ambulance on 101 (Magen David Adom). If you have sudden shortness of breath, chest pain, fainting, or severe difficulty breathing, call an ambulance right away at 101 (Magen David Adom): after the procedure, such symptoms can point to a dangerous complication, including an embolism. Also contact the surgical ward or seek medical care urgently for new severe back pain, fever, spreading redness, swelling, or discharge from the puncture site.

026	Neck surgery from the front (ACDF)
027	Anterior cervical discectomy and fusion (ACDF) is one of the most common operations for a compressed nerve root in the neck. Through a small cut at the front of the neck, the surgeon removes the damaged disc and, if needed, bone spurs, puts a bone graft or spacer in its place, and often holds the vertebrae with a plate and screws. The vertebrae gradually fuse.
028	Surgery is considered if pain, numbness or weakness in the arm continues or gets worse despite treatment without surgery. Increasing weakness in the arms, problems with walking or other signs of spinal cord involvement need urgent assessment, without waiting for that treatment to finish. For neck pain alone, surgery usually does not help.
029	There are other options too, such as an artificial disc or surgery from the back of the neck; your surgeon decides which is right for you.
030	Preparation (including showering)
031	Fasting, showering and skin preparation follow the general leaflet "Preparing for surgery". If you smoke, talk to your doctor about stopping. Before surgery, let your family know that in the first days your voice may be hoarse and talking may be hard.
032	Do not stop blood thinners or other regular medicines yourself — your doctor will tell you which ones to stop and when. Details are in the leaflet "Preparing for surgery".
033	Sometimes a thin tube (drain) is left in the wound to let extra fluid out. It is not always used — the surgeon decides. It is usually removed after a short time, once little fluid is coming out.
034	Usually within the first day you start getting up and walking with the team's permission; you can eat and drink when it is allowed and swallowing is safe. Many people go home after one or two days.
035	In the first weeks it is often hard to swallow solid food and your voice may be hoarse — this usually passes. Eat sitting upright, slowly, in small amounts.
036	Collar, driving, lifting and returning to work follow your surgeon's instructions. Fusion of the vertebrae takes several months.
